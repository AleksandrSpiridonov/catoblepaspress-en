*The launch of Bias Tape, 24 September 2026, Saint Petersburg. This report was first published [on the press’s Telegram channel](https://t.me/catoblepaspress/265) on 2 October 2026. Information about availability and publishing plans reflects that date; for current details, see [[published/biastape|the book page]]. The videos retain their original Russian audio.*

Thursday, 24 September 2026. In Moscow, the second Art Russia. Classics fair of academic art is opening, and the seventh blazar fair is beginning at the Museum of Moscow. Saint Petersburg, despite its customary weather, is hosting the XII International United Cultures Forum. Meanwhile, in the Benois quarter, not far from St Nicholas Naval Cathedral, at 39 Rimsky-Korsakov Avenue — through the second archway, in the basement of a courtyard wing of the former Secretariat of State of the Grand Duchy of Finland, now occupied by the self-organised art space [F5 / Noise Schedule](https://t.me/f5noisegraph) — the launch of Marusya Navka’s poetry collection Bias Tape begins at nine in the evening.

You can certainly get lost: try a door marked “Shelter No. 1”, for instance, or knock at CDEK to ask how to get to the launch. But after a few laps of the courtyard, you may work out that the open door beneath the ramp, barely visible in the dark, is the very place described on the event poster as “on the right — a door”.

F5 itself is an exceptional place in Eastern Europe’s cultural landscape. Last year, the festival “Suffering Aborigines of the Age of Technofeudalism” brought together ambient, free jazz, and noise. This year, you could hear the IK-20 improvisation circle, a happening for violins and cassette recorders at “воблости в августе”, the noise-industrial project Prospekt Veteranov, the jazz groove of [Tundra Art Ensemble](https://music.yandex.ru/artist/8990793), the post-punk/darkwave/industrial/noise band [chernoch](https://music.yandex.ru/artist/21071140), cassette-loop improvisations by [Posledniy Izgnannik](https://music.yandex.ru/artist/21283931), and the trio [the popcorners!](https://music.yandex.ru/artist/24194594). The noise schedule has been agreed with the neighbours! F5 also hosted “Vault”, an exhibition by ceramic artist [Valeria Bondarenko](https://povedaii.com/profile), in 2022; the group exhibition “I’m 20”, curated by Anna Kulminskaya, in 2025; and, in April 2026, a group exhibition by the venue’s residents as part of the three-day festival “F5 Collection of Curiosities”. As for poetry, July brought [Normal Poems](https://t.me/norm_stihi_hi_hi) and [Poetry Here and Now](https://t.me/poesiazdes), for example.

After some serious face control, Marusya signs a book for everyone herself. One hundred numbered copies in all. Nearby, the professional crew of Monastyring, a documentary from VGIK, is getting ready to film: since late summer, they have been following Marusya’s stay at a monastery as a lay worker. Tonight’s event is the film’s epilogue. Once again, we thank them for helping to film our interview with Marusya, now available [on our YouTube channel](https://youtu.be/zy-AiSZowQw), with visual design by Anastasia Achkasova.

At ten, the supergroup [ZAGS](https://music.yandex.ru/artist/26368864) (Untalented Label) begins to play; drums and bass fill the whole courtyard. If you arrive late, you have to stand by the entrance, behind a pillar, to one side of the musicians — which may be a decent refuge from the decibels. On drums, mostly two toms, stands Mishanya Sergeevich, who made the wonderful illustrations for Bias Tape and its cover: the original, stitched with yellow thread, exists in a single copy. On bass is Sanchez, a Mexican musician, founder of the Ekhomaniya festival and [Untalented Label](https://t.me/untalented_label), whose support helped bring the book into print.

> a minimalist performance in guerrilla conditions, underscoring the author’s poetic stance; like the author’s poems, the launch was imbued with the spirit of New Sincerity, a deliberate departure from settled ideas about how a book launch should happen

> I was just delighted. I liked everything so much. I think I had a great time. Even though I felt like shit because I’d barely slept. The way the musicians played, the way they looked, the music, the words, the mix of instruments, the atmosphere, the place. We all really liked it. And yeah, I especially liked the format. I liked that it wasn’t a big concert in some club, but more of a house gig. As I understood it, most people there knew the musicians and the author in one way or another, and you’d invited them yourselves? Or were there people who’d just come in off the street? No, there was definitely one guy from the street. He met me and Danya while we were standing outside the entrance.

> It was my first time at this kind of event. The setting felt a little like home because people seemed “close”, although I didn’t know anyone. While ZAGS was playing and afterwards, your way of connecting with people felt simple and open, like a conversation — that’s lovely)

A house belonging to the architect Vasily Bazhenov once stood on this site. The new building dates from 1808–1810; Harald Bosse extended it in 1845. Our courtyard wing, meanwhile, was built in 1908. From 1826, the building — not the wing, which did not yet exist — housed institutions of the Grand Duchy of Finland. From 1923 to 1938, it housed Finland’s Consulate General, and in 1954 the building was sold to the USSR. It has been listed as a cultural heritage site since 2001. The original cobblestones were supposed to have survived in the courtyard, but I think an ambulance was parked on them.

> After going to the Bias Tape launch, I was left with a feeling of dissonance: the music of ZAGS and the F5 space didn’t fit together into a single puzzle. On the other hand, maybe that mismatch is precisely the point — something I think is characteristic of Marusya.
